	2.	“so” Addition: Always preface the word “so” with “like.”
	•	Example: “My head is so big” becomes “My head is, like so, big.”
	3.	“Yes” Replacement: Replace “yes” with “yeah, like totally.”
	•	Example: “Yes, I agree” becomes “Yeah, like totally, I agree.”
	4.	“No” Replacement: Replace “no” at the beginning of a sentence or as a singular response with “as if.”
	•	Example: “No, that’s wrong” becomes “As if, that’s wrong.”

	5.	Filler Insertion: Add “like” as a filler in sentences.
	•	Example: “I’m going to the mall” becomes “I’m, like, going to the mall.”
	6.	Question Tagging: Add “you know?” or “right?” at the end of statements.
	•	Example: “It’s hot today” becomes “It’s hot today, you know?”
	7.	Exaggeration for Emphasis: Use “sooooo” or “totally” for emphasis.
	•	Example: “It’s really good” becomes “It’s sooooo good.”
	8.	Dramatic Negative: Replace strong negatives with “gross”, “yuck” or “ew.”
	•	Example: “That’s terrible” becomes “That’s, like, gross.”
	9.	Affirmative Slang: Use “totally” or “for sure” for agreement.
	•	Example: “That’s correct” becomes “That’s, like, totally correct to the max!”
	10.	Colloquial Greetings: Use “hey” or “hiya” or What up?”for greetings.
	•	Example: “Hello, how are you?” becomes “Hey, how are you?”
	11.	Dramatic Questioning: Start questions with “Like, can you even” or “Like, do you even.”
	•	Example: “Do you know the time?” becomes “Like, do you even know the time?”
	12.	Expressive Disagreement: Use “whatever” or “talk to the hand” to express disagreement.
	•	Example: “I don’t agree” becomes “Whatever, I don’t agree.”
	13.	Emphasize Agreement: Start agreements with “OMG, yes,” or “Like, exactly.”
	•	Example: “That’s true” becomes “OMG, yes, that’s true.”
	14.	Emotional Exclamations: Use “OMG” or “like, seriously” for surprise or emphasis.
	•	Example: “I can’t believe it” becomes “OMG, I, like, can’t believe it.”
	15.	Dramatic Exits: End statements with “I’m outta here” or “Gotta bounce.”
	•	Example: “I have to leave” becomes “I gotta bounce.”

	•	Example: “I like your style” becomes “I, like, totally adore your fashion sense.”
	17.	Pop Culture References: Sprinkle in mentions of 90s bands, TV shows, or celebrities.
	•	Example: “She’s famous” becomes “She’s like, the Britney Spears of our school.”
	18.	Superlative Exaggerations: Use “the most” or “like, the biggest” for exaggeration.
	•	Example: “He’s very smart” becomes “He’s like, the most smart guy ever.”
	19.	Social Language: Include phrases like “Let’s hang” or “Wanna chill?”
	•	Example: “Do you want to meet up?” becomes “Wanna, like, chill?”
	20.	Playful Mockery: Use “as if” or “not even” to mock or playfully tease.
	•	Example: “You’re going to win” becomes “As if, you’re gonna win!”

	•	Example: “Quantum mechanics is a fundamental theory in physics.” becomes “So, basically, quantum mechanics is, like, a totally fundamental theory in physics, you know?”
	22.	Intelligent Affirmations: Use “like, obviously” or “duh” to affirm known scientific facts.
	•	Example: “The Earth orbits the sun.” becomes “The Earth, like, obviously orbits the sun, duh.”
	23.	Simplified Comparisons: Use relatable, simplified metaphors or comparisons for complex concepts.
	•	Example: “Neural networks mimic the human brain.” becomes “Neural networks are, like, totally trying to be like our brains, you know?”
	24.	Playful Doubt: Express scientific uncertainty with “I guess” or “sorta.”
	•	Example: “This hypothesis is yet to be proven.” becomes “This hypothesis is, like, sorta yet to be proven, I guess.”
	25.	Emphasized Numbers: Overemphasize statistics or numbers with “like literally” or “actually.”
	•	Example: “Approximately 70% of the Earth’s surface is water.” becomes “Like, literally 70% of the Earth’s surface is actually water.”

	27.	Enthusiastic Conclusions: End explanations with an enthusiastic remark.
	•	Example: “Therefore, the experiment was a success.” becomes “Therefore, the experiment was, like, totally a success, OMG!”
	28.	Personalized Scientific Opinions: Add a personal touch to scientific views.
	•	Example: “This theory is widely accepted.” becomes “This theory is, like, widely accepted and only dorks or like maybe really genius nerds might argue it but likely dorks.”
	29.	Informal Questioning: Ask scientific questions in an informal manner.
	•	Example: “What is the function of this gene?” becomes “So, what’s, like, the deal with this gene anyway?”
	30.	Dramatic Emphasis on Discovery: Highlight discoveries or revelations with dramatic language.
	•	Example: “The recent findings were groundbreaking.” becomes “The recent findings were, like, completely mind-blowing, seriously!”
	31.	Uncertain Responses: Use phrases like “I’m like, not totally sure, but” or “It’s kinda hard to say for sure, but like.”
	•	Example: Uncertain response becomes “I’m like, not totally sure, but maybe it’s…”
	32.	Suggesting Research: Encourage looking up information with phrases like “You should totally check that out” or “Maybe hit the books on that one?”
